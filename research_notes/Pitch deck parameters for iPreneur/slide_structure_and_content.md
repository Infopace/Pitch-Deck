# Canonical Investor Pitch Deck Structure and Slide Content (2025–2026)

Research notes for encoding iPreneur's deck-generator section definitions. Scope: US VC norms with a global view. India-specific norms are covered elsewhere.

Source-quality legend used below:
- **[Primary]**: the investor or firm's own publication.
- **[Data]**: platform analytics (DocSend, Papermark, Storydoc), with sample and year noted.
- **[Secondary]**: journalist or practitioner summary.
- **[Vendor]**: deck-tool or agency blog. Treat as opinion, not evidence.
- **Pre-2020** marks older guidance, with a note on whether it still holds.

Access notes: docsend.com and guykawasaki.com returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches. The ycombinator.com/library pages rendered without body text. DocSend and the YC library are therefore cited from search-result excerpts of the original pages or from summaries that link to them, and each such citation says so.

## Q1. What do the major published frameworks prescribe, and where do they agree or disagree?

### Takeaway
Every major framework shares the same core of about 10 slides: purpose or one-liner, problem, solution, market, business model, competition, team, and either the ask or financials. They differ on four things:
- whether "why now" and "vision" get their own slides (Sequoia: yes)
- whether traction is a standalone slide (YC, CRV, DocSend: yes; Sequoia's classic list has none)
- how much financial projection belongs at seed (Kawasaki and TechCrunch: include it; YC: little meaningful detail exists at seed)
- whether there is an explicit "unique insight" slide (YC and Seibel: yes; Kawasaki's version is "underlying magic")

### Cited Findings

**Sequoia Capital ("Writing a business plan") [Primary]**
- The 10 sections, in order:
  1. Company purpose: one declarative sentence that states the mission.
  2. Problem: the customer's pain, how it is handled today, and the shortcomings of current solutions.
  3. Solution: the key insight, why the value proposition is unique and compelling, why it will last, and where it goes next.
  4. Why now: why the solution could not have been built earlier.
  5. Market potential: the customer and the market. Some companies create their own markets.
  6. Competition/alternatives: direct and indirect competitors, plus a plan to win.
  7. Business model.
  8. Team: the founders' story and key team members.
  9. Financials: "if you have any."
  10. Vision: what you will have built in five years.
  — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- The page sets no length or format rule and links to a slide template. It currently shows a date of Oct 21, 2026, which probably reflects republishing; this guidance has circulated for well over a decade. Note that the list has no dedicated traction slide and no ask slide. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)

**Y Combinator: Aaron Harris, "How to build your seed round pitch deck" [Primary, body not rendered; quoted via search excerpts; undated, likely pre-2020]**
- Title slide: "the name of your company, and a one line description of what you do. This is the only place in the deck where you can only have 1 slide." — [YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/2u-how-to-build-your-seed-round-pitch-deck) (via search excerpt)
- Problem slide: "clearly state the problem," with "particulars of how this problem impacts real world people." — [YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/2u-how-to-build-your-seed-round-pitch-deck) (via search excerpt)
- Template order per secondary commentary: Problem → Solution → Traction → Revenue → Business Model → Market & Growth → Team. — [Convinco](https://www.convinco.co/blog/how-to-build-your-yc-style-pitch-deck); template file at [Eloquens](https://www.eloquens.com/tool/y5BF1q/startups/pitch-decks/the-yc-seed-deck-template)
- Harris warns: "there isn't very much meaningful detail to explore for most seed stage companies. When founders pretend that there is, their stories get muddled." His principle is "Focus on narrative. The rest is commentary." — [YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/2u-how-to-build-your-seed-round-pitch-deck) (via search excerpt)

**YC: Michael Seibel, "How to pitch your company" [Primary, undated, originally on themacro.com, likely pre-2020; still widely taught]**
- The pitch is built on seven questions, in order:
  1. What do you do (one-liner)
  2. Market size
  3. Progress (traction)
  4. Unique insight
  5. Business model
  6. Team
  7. The ask
  — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- He calls the unique insight "similar to 'What problem are you solving?' but the bar is higher." There is no separate problem or solution section. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)

**YC: Geoff Ralston, "A Guide to Demo Day Presentations" (July 25, 2016) [Primary, pre-2020]**
- Components: Introduction → Problem → Product and customer → Opportunity (bottom-up market) → Traction → Team → Conclusion. Order is flexible. The business model can be brief, but investors should believe you have one. — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Four "vertebrae" questions to find the 3–4 key points:
  - What are we building and for whom?
  - Why hasn't this been done before?
  - Why is it hard?
  - Why is this an opportunity not to be missed?
  — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)

**Guy Kawasaki: 10/20/30 rule [Primary, pre-2020: 2005, restated 2015]**
- The rule: 10 slides, 20 minutes, minimum 30-point font. Kawasaki's rationale is that ten slides is what the mind can handle. — [AhaSlides summary](https://ahaslides.com/blog/10-20-30-rule-presentations); original at [guykawasaki.com](https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/) (direct fetch returned 403)
- His ten slides, as widely reproduced (not verified this session because of the 403):
  1. Problem
  2. Solution/value proposition
  3. Business model
  4. Underlying magic/technology
  5. Marketing and sales
  6. Competition
  7. Team
  8. Financial projections and key metrics (3–5 years)
  9. Status, accomplishments, timeline, and use of funds
  10. Summary and call to action
  — [guykawasaki.com](https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/) (see Gaps)

**CRV, "The Seed Pitch Deck Guide" (Aug 20, 2026) [Primary, current]**
- Order (described as "flexible"):
  1. Cover: name, logo, plain-language line, stage, raise amount.
  2. Problem: with a number on the cost.
  3. Solution.
  4. Product: real screenshots or a demo.
  5. Market size: bottom-up TAM/SAM/SOM.
  6. Business model: pricing and its link to unit economics.
  7. Traction.
  8. Competition.
  9. Team.
  10. The ask: amount, use of funds, milestones.
  — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Most seed decks run 10–12 slides, one idea per slide. CRV's target is "the shortest deck that makes the argument clearly." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)

**DocSend (Dropbox) seed guide, updated Mar 2026 [Data and Primary; cited via third-party excerpts because of the 403]**
- "Sealing the deal in 12 slides":
  1. Company purpose
  2. Problem
  3. Solution
  4. Market size
  5. Why now (optional)
  6. Product
  7. Competition
  8. Traction
  9. Team
  10. Business model
  11. Financials (optional)
  12. Fundraising ask
  — [DocSend seed guide](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/) (order as reproduced in search excerpt and [Kruze](https://kruzeconsulting.com/blog/top-5-venture-capital-pitch-decks/))
- The same guide recommends a 19–20 page seed deck and reports 3m44s average review time and 58% completion. The 19–20 figure is a recommendation, not a measured outcome. — [Hummingdeck summary of DocSend](https://hummingdeck.com/blog/pitch-deck-benchmarks-2026)

**TechCrunch (Haje Jan Kamps, Aug 30, 2023) [Secondary, informed by DocSend data]**
- A 16-slide list:
  1. Intro
  2. Mission
  3. Problem
  4. Solution
  5. Product/demo
  6. Value proposition
  7. Market opportunity
  8. Business model
  9. Go-to-market
  10. Traction
  11. Competition
  12. Financial projections
  13. Team
  14. Funding ask
  15. Use of funds
  16. Thank you/Q&A/contact
  Kamps says the order is not fixed: lead with what is most unique. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)

**Reid Hoffman, LinkedIn Series B (2004 deck, annotated 2013) [Secondary write-ups of a primary post; pre-2020, still widely cited]**
- Open with the investment thesis: what investors must believe to want to be shareholders.
- Lead with one revenue stream, not several.
- Include explicit risk factors and their mitigations. Hoffman later wished he had.
- He called it a "concept pitch" because the data at the time "wasn't impressive."
— [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/billionaire-reid-hoffman-reveals-linkedins-2004-pitch-deck/229469); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2013/10/reid-hoffman-linkedin-pitch-deck-10-pieces-advice)

**Hustle Fund (Elizabeth Yin) [Primary interview and podcast]**
- She prefers written pitches because they are "faster to process." "Whether it's a Notion page... or a deck, I don't care." A 10-minute founder video is "challenging" to watch. — [Mercury interview](https://blog.mercury.com/series-tea/elizabeth-yin)
- The initial pitch email should be about five bullets: problem, solution, traction, market size, team. — [SaaS Club podcast summary](https://saasclub.io/podcast/saas-fundraising-elizabeth-yin-hustle-fund/)

### Inferences
- The universal core is: one-liner/purpose, problem, solution, product, market, business model, traction, competition, team, ask. Sequoia's omission of traction and the ask reflects its "business plan" heritage. Every current, data-informed source (CRV 2026, DocSend 2026, TC 2023) includes both, so a generator should treat them as mandatory.
- "Why now," "vision," "unique insight," "GTM," and "risks" are the main optional or contested slides. Each has a credible champion: Sequoia for why now and vision, YC for insight, Kawasaki and TC for GTM, Hoffman for risks. They are good candidates for the extra slides that take iPreneur from about 12 to 20+ slides.

### Gaps
- **a16z, Founders Fund, First Round Review, NFX, Point Nine:** no primary deck-structure guidance from these firms was retrieved. Searches returned only aggregator summaries. One Medium post ("What should be in my fundraising slides?") says it was written with Point Nine input, but it was not fetched. — [Medium/The Stack](https://medium.com/the-stack/what-should-be-in-my-fundraising-slides-eca162d14d2a)
- **Kawasaki's slide list:** not verified this session (403). The list above reproduces the well-known original. Verify before quoting.
- **YC seed-deck body text:** the full slide-by-slide text of Aaron Harris's article could not be rendered. Only excerpts were obtained.

## Q2. What must each core slide contain, what are the common mistakes, and what makes it convincing?

### Takeaway
Investors reward specificity: a quantified problem, a bottom-up market, labeled growth with a stated conclusion, one business model, and an ask tied to dated milestones. They penalize vagueness: buzzwords, top-down "1% of a huge market" math, rigged 2x2s, cumulative charts, and fuzzy asks. Team, financials, business model, and traction are where reading time concentrates.

### Cited Findings

**Where attention goes**
- DocSend 2015 (200+ decks, $360M raised): investors spent an average of 3m44s per deck. The most-studied slides were financials, team, and competition, in that order. — [TechCrunch, June 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/06/08/lessons-from-a-study-of-perfect-pitch-decks-vcs-spend-an-average-of-3-minutes-44-seconds-on-them/)
- DocSend 2021 seed report: in successful decks, investors spent 94% more time on the business model section, 236% more time on financials, and 78% *less* time on traction than in unsuccessful decks. Average time on a successful deck's business model section was 31 seconds. — [PR Newswire, Sep 22, 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/show-us-the-momentum-new-docsend-report-reveals-vcs-pushing-seed-startups-on-business-model-traction-and-financials-301382159.html)
- 2024 per-section times attributed to DocSend: Team 1m02s, Financials 52s, Traction 49s, Problem 38s, Product about 21s. **[Secondary only; not verified against DocSend.]** — [PitchGrade](https://pitchgrade.com/blog/what-investors-read-pitch-deck-docsend-data)
- An older DocSend post found the team slide was the only slide present in every successful deck. — [DocSend seed guide via search excerpt](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/)
- Papermark (about 3,000 decks, 2024 data): the first page gets 23 seconds and pages 2–10 get about 15 seconds each. — [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/pitch-deck-metrics)

**1. Title / one-liner (cover)**
- Must contain:
  - Company name and a one-line description of what you do (YC Harris). — [YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/2u-how-to-build-your-seed-round-pitch-deck)
  - Name, logo, a plain-language line, stage, and raise amount (CRV). — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
  - Sequoia's equivalent is one declarative sentence for company purpose. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- Mistake: "A cover that makes someone guess what you build has already lost time you cannot spare." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Mistake: jargon, acronyms, marketing language, and vague words like "platform."
- Seibel's "Email Test": write two sentences, email them to a smart friend, and have them explain it back. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- Plain-language example: "We deliver groceries to customers in their homes." — [YC Blog, Ralston 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)

**2. Problem**
- Must contain:
  - The customer's pain, how it is addressed today, and the shortcomings of current solutions. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
  - "Particulars of how this problem impacts real world people." — [YC Library](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/2u-how-to-build-your-seed-round-pitch-deck)
  - A specific, documented pain tied to a real buyer, "with a number on the cost." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Mistake: letting the problem section run so long that listeners don't learn what you do until late. — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)

**3. Solution and unique insight**
- Must contain: the key insight, why the value proposition is unique and compelling, why it will last, and where it goes next. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- Unique insight = what you know that others don't, usually drawn from customer conversations, competitive analysis, or personal experience. "A unique insight is specific and doesn't contain complicated language." A weak insight stated passionately makes you look worse. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- CRV: "the concrete product answer, explicitly tied to the problem." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)

**4. Why now**
- Must contain: why the timing is right and why it couldn't have been built earlier. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- YC phrasing: "Why hasn't this been done before?" and "Why is it hard to do what we are doing?" — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- DocSend lists why now as optional. A practitioner summary calls it "often skipped" despite high impact. — [DocSend seed guide via excerpt](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/); [Waveup (Vendor)](https://waveup.com/blog/what-to-include-in-a-pitch-deck/)

**5. Product / demo**
- Must contain: how it works, through real screenshots, a working prototype, or a live demo. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- **Conflict for presenting decks:**
  - Kevin Hale (YC, Nov 2015, pre-2020) says avoid screenshots, screencasts, and videos ("almost always illegible, complex, and non-obvious") and use a short bulleted list of steps instead. — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)
  - Ralston: videos are "almost never a good idea" and demos "seldom work in modern demo day presentations." — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
  - Resolution: screenshots suit read/send-ahead decks (CRV); simplified visuals suit projected decks (YC).
- Mistake: "Most founders spend too much time on their product story." — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)

**6. Market size (TAM/SAM/SOM)**
- Unanimous guidance is to build bottom-up:
  - Seibel: for new categories, estimate how many customers would want it and what they'd pay. Top-down estimates skip narrowing the segment. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
  - Ralston: bottom-up "X * Y" (price × potential customers). — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
  - CRV: "average revenue per customer multiplied by the number of real customers in your segment." "Those top-down numbers read as hollow without your own bottom-up work." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Common mistakes:
  - "1% of a huge TAM" math. — [Waveup (Vendor)](https://waveup.com/blog/what-to-include-in-a-pitch-deck/)
  - Assuming the whole market is the customer base. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- Sequoia adds that some strong companies create their own markets. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- Contrast, the Airbnb 2009 deck (secondary teardowns): it used a top-down framing, 2B+ trips booked yearly with capture of a share yielding 84M trips/year, plus CouchSurfing (≈500–630K users; sources conflict) as demand validation. Today's guidance would push this toward bottom-up. — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startups-airbnb-pitch-deck); [VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/airbnb-pitch-deck-original-2009-analysis)

**7. Business model**
- Must contain: pricing, revenue type, and how it ties to unit economics. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Pick one simple model. Don't list a mix. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- "One revenue stream will drive the business." — [Entrepreneur on Hoffman](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/billionaire-reid-hoffman-reveals-linkedins-2004-pitch-deck/229469)
- Business model gets 94% more time in successful seed decks, so it is heavily scrutinized. — [PR Newswire / DocSend 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/show-us-the-momentum-new-docsend-report-reveals-vcs-pushing-seed-startups-on-business-model-traction-and-financials-301382159.html)

**8. Traction**
- Must contain:
  - "Show us your startup growth curves." — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
  - "Your strongest proof of growth, revenue, retention, or usage." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
  - Seibel values product development and customers over fundraising or business development deals, framed as work produced relative to time spent. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- Pre-revenue proof (DocSend): more than one type of traction, such as LOIs, testimonials, a defined pipeline, or beta feedback. — [DocSend seed guide via excerpt](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/)
- Mistakes:
  - Cumulative graphs "always cause immediate suspicion." — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
  - Unlabeled or cherry-picked traction. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
  - Graphs without a stated conclusion. Write the conclusion as a caption or headline. — [YC Blog, Hale](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)
- Investors spent 78% *more* time on traction in unsuccessful decks, plausibly because weak or confusing traction draws scrutiny. — [PR Newswire / DocSend 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/show-us-the-momentum-new-docsend-report-reveals-vcs-pushing-seed-startups-on-business-model-traction-and-financials-301382159.html)

**9. Go-to-market**
- Kawasaki has "marketing and sales." TechCrunch has "how it will acquire customers." — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)
- Elizabeth Yin's reported signature question: "How exactly will you get your first hundred customers?" **[Third-party profile; treat as secondhand.]** — [Gatekeep](https://gatekeep.vc/vcs/3c734a5e-ccb6-4c03-af18-93737c4177e3)
- Airbnb's "adoption" slide named concrete channels, for example cross-posting listings to Craigslist. — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startups-airbnb-pitch-deck)

**10. Competition / positioning**
- Must contain: direct and indirect competitors and a plan to win. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- CRV: "a two-axis matrix or a feature comparison table." Lead with a one-sentence claim for why you win, and name competitors. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- **2x2 vs. feature matrix (practitioner consensus) [Vendor]:**
  - Use a 2x2 when the advantage is positional and the axes reflect real buyer trade-offs.
  - Use a table when the edge is a bundle of capabilities.
  - Warnings:
    - rigged axes that put your logo alone top-right
    - "Price vs. Features" axes
    - feature checklists, which are "copyable in 6 months"
    - attacking rivals
  - Durable advantages cited instead: regulatory position, network effects, proprietary data.
  — [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-make-a-winning-competition-slide-for-your-pitch-deck/); [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/competitor-analysis-pitch-deck); [Ink Narrates](https://www.inknarrates.com/post/pitch-deck-competition-slide)
- Hale's exception: a deliberately dense competitor grid (Magic) worked because its goal was to convey difficulty. — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)

**11. Moat / defensibility**
- No major framework gives the moat its own slide. Sequoia folds "why it will last" into Solution. CRV folds "why customers choose you" into Competition. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/); [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Airbnb used a separate "competitive advantages" slide after competition. — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startups-airbnb-pitch-deck)

**12. Team**
- Must contain:
  - The founders' story and key team members. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
  - Seibel's specifics: number of founders, whether there is a technical co-founder, how long the founders have known each other, whether everyone is full-time, and the equity split. Add a relevant credential, especially in complex or regulated fields. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
  - CRV: "Relevant prior experience tied to this specific problem." "Team quality consistently ranks as the single most important factor in early stage decisions." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Ralston: sell the team only with truly notable facts. — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Hale: "Investors invest in teams not slides." — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)

**13. Financials / projections**
- Sequoia: include them "if you have any." — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/)
- CRV puts financial assumptions in the appendix. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Kamps: "I like to leave the spreadsheets in spreadsheets." Summarize growth in an operating plan. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)
- Financials drew 236% more time in successful 2020/21 seed decks, and were the most-studied slide in DocSend's 2015 data. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/show-us-the-momentum-new-docsend-report-reveals-vcs-pushing-seed-startups-on-business-model-traction-and-financials-301382159.html); [TechCrunch 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/06/08/lessons-from-a-study-of-perfect-pitch-decks-vcs-spend-an-average-of-3-minutes-44-seconds-on-them/)
- Front's 2016 Series A deck dedicated a slide to capital efficiency (spend vs. ARR added) and highlighted net retention (≈120% at 6 months, 150% at 1 year). It also openly flagged gaps such as missing projections. **[Secondary teardowns; details conflict.]** — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/front-series-saas-startup-pitch-deck/); [MyPitchDecks](https://mypitchdecks.com/case-studies/front-pitch-deck)

**14. The ask and use of funds**
- Must contain: one specific amount, use of funds broken into "a small set of categories, each tied to a dated milestone." Frame it "around the next risk you will remove instead of the months you want to survive." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- TechCrunch adds instrument type and prior funding, with use of funds on a separate slide. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)
- Mistakes:
  - "A fuzzy ask says you have not mapped money to milestones." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
  - Vague asks like "What do you think?" Seibel: "Make it easy for me to help you." — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)

**15. Roadmap / milestones**
- Usually merged with the ask: "the milestones it buys." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Kawasaki has a "status, accomplishments, timeline" slide. — [guykawasaki.com](https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/) (unverified, 403)
- One practitioner list includes a standalone roadmap slide before the ask. — [Waveup (Vendor)](https://waveup.com/blog/what-to-include-in-a-pitch-deck/)

**16. Vision**
- Sequoia: "what you will have built in five years" if things go well. It is the only framework with a dedicated vision slide. TechCrunch's "mission" slide is the closest analogue. — [Sequoia](https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/); [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)

**17. Risks**
- Hoffman: explicitly identify the risks that could thwart success and how you will mitigate them. — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2013/10/reid-hoffman-linkedin-pitch-deck-10-pieces-advice)

**18. Conclusion / summary**
- Tell listeners what to remember, often by restating the 3–4 vertebrae. — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Kawasaki's slide 10 is "summary and call to action." — [guykawasaki.com](https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/) (unverified)

**19. Appendix**
- Typical contents: long customer testimonials, detailed GTM or IP strategy, full financial spreadsheets. — [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/18/pitch-deck-contexts/)
- CRV: keep it short, holding financial assumptions and deeper cohort or technical detail. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)

**Cross-slide design rules**
- "One main point, never more." "More than 7 words on a slide... is likely to be too many." Use high contrast and put important text at the top. — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Slides should be "legible, simple, and obvious." Test: show a slide to a stranger, and "If they don't immediately say your idea, you lose." — [YC Blog, Hale](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)
- Presentation decks: at most about 10 words per slide and text of at least 35 pt. — [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/18/pitch-deck-contexts/)
- CRV mistakes list: walls of text, 40-slide decks, top-down-only market math, a vague ask, no through-line, unlabeled traction, "buzzword soup and undefended AI claims." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Ralston: exaggerations are "fatal errors." Testimonials have "almost no impact." — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)

### Inferences
- **Generator rules that follow directly from the sources:**
  - Every slide gets an assertion headline (a sentence stating the conclusion), not a topic label like "Market."
  - Every chart needs a caption with the takeaway, and growth should be shown as non-cumulative periods.
  - The market slide's calculation should be (number of reachable customers in segment) × (ARPU), with TAM/SAM/SOM shown as derived values and the assumptions listed.
  - The competition slide should require a one-sentence "why we win" claim plus either a 2x2 with non-trivial axes or a benefits-based table, and should name indirect alternatives, including "do nothing" or spreadsheets.
  - The ask slide should require amount, instrument, runway, and 3–5 use-of-funds categories, each tied to a dated milestone.
- **Weighting by scrutiny:** team, business model, financials, and traction get the most scrutiny. These sections deserve the richest prompt templates and data validation in iPreneur, for example flagging unlabeled axes, cumulative charts, or a missing equity or full-time status.
- The testimonial conflict (Airbnb 2009 included them; Ralston 2016 says they have "almost no impact" on stage) suggests testimonials belong in the read or send-ahead deck or the appendix, not in a presented deck.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 DocSend per-section timing data was retrievable. The 2024 per-section numbers are secondary only.
- No quantitative evidence was found that a 2x2 outperforms a feature matrix. That guidance is practitioner opinion.
- No primary source was found that specifies a standalone "moat" or "roadmap" slide as required.

## Q3. What is the evidence-based slide count for each deck type, and how do investors view 20–30 slide decks?

### Takeaway
- **Send-ahead or reading deck:** about 12–20 pages. DocSend recommends 19–20 for seed. 2023 data shows the average shrinking to 16, and successful pre-seed decks run shorter than unsuccessful ones.
- **Presenting deck:** about 10–12 slides (Kawasaki, CRV).
- **Demo Day:** 5–7 slides (YC 2015; current format unverified).
- **Data room or appendix:** effectively unbounded. Rippling sent 46 slides of metrics alongside a memo.
- **20–30 slides:** the evidence leans against a 20–30-slide *main narrative*. That length is defensible only as a roughly 12–18-slide core plus a clearly separated appendix.

### Cited Findings
- **DocSend 2015** (200+ decks that raised $360M): successful decks averaged about 19–20 pages (secondary sources say "around 19" and "20"). — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/pitch-deck-analysis-from-200-startups-pitchdecks-that-raised-360m/); [Pitchwall](https://pitchwall.co/blog/what-200-funded-pitch-decks-have-in-common). TechCrunch's write-up separately mentions "10 slides to include in a seed deck." — [TechCrunch 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/06/08/lessons-from-a-study-of-perfect-pitch-decks-vcs-spend-an-average-of-3-minutes-44-seconds-on-them/)
- **DocSend 2023** (via TechCrunch):
  - average deck length fell from 19 slides to 16
  - time per deck fell from 2m42s to 2m12s
  - founders sent 16% more decks
  - 40% of successful pre-seed teams had shorter-than-average decks
  - 65% of unsuccessful teams had more slides than average
  - Kamps: "Founders have to be ruthless, both with the number of slides in a deck and the number of words on each slide." Headline: "That 30-slide deck won't cut it anymore."
  — [TechCrunch, Aug 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)
- **DocSend 2023 pre-seed** (secondary): successful decks averaged 16 slides vs. 19 for unsuccessful ones. — [Pynn](https://pynn.ai/blog/what-investors-read-in-your-deck-and-how-long-you-have)
- **DocSend seed guide** (updated Mar 2026): recommends 19–20 pages, 3m44s average review, 58% completion. **DocSend pre-seed guide** (Feb 2026): 4m10s average review, and a 1–2% deck-to-meeting rate. — [Hummingdeck summary](https://hummingdeck.com/blog/pitch-deck-benchmarks-2026), linking [DocSend seed](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/) and [DocSend pre-seed](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-to-include-when-building-your-pre-seed-pitch-deck/)
- **DocSend weekly index:** average time per deck was about 2m18s in early 2024, after holding around 2m30s for a period. **[Secondary report of DocSend index; conflicting figures across sources.]** — [DocSend Startup Index](https://www.docsend.com/pitch-deck-metrics/) (via search excerpt)
- **Papermark** (about 3,000 decks, 2024 data):
  - most common length 9–16 pages (49% of decks); 17–24 pages second most common
  - 3.2 minutes average complete review; about 15 seconds per page after page 1
  - no outcome correlation reported
  — [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/pitch-deck-metrics)
- **Storydoc** (Dec 2025, interactive-deck platform):
  - engagement falls after 18 slides
  - 31% of sessions end within 10 seconds
  - decks around 10 slides show 32% completion
  - engagement measure only; platform-specific
  — [Hummingdeck summary of Storydoc](https://hummingdeck.com/blog/pitch-deck-benchmarks-2026)
- **CRV 2026:** most seed decks run 10–12 slides. "40-slide decks" are listed as a mistake. — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- **Kawasaki:** 10 slides for a 20-minute presentation. — [AhaSlides](https://ahaslides.com/blog/10-20-30-rule-presentations) **[pre-2020; the ~10-slide presenting norm still matches CRV 2026]**
- **Demo Day:** Hale (Nov 2015) describes picking 5–7 key ideas and about 5–7 slides for a 2m30s slot. — [YC Blog](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck) **[pre-2020]** Secondary 2026 guides claim a 1-minute format or 6 on-stage slides. **[Unverified; not from YC.]** — [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-demo-day-presentation-format-15-slide-script-that-wins-checks)
- **Teaser deck:** 3–4 slides. **Presentation deck:** at most 10 words per slide, text at least 35 pt. — [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/18/pitch-deck-contexts/)
- **Data-room or appendix scale:** Rippling raised a $45M Series A with no formal pitch deck, using an investor memo plus 46 slides of metrics, projections, and methodology footnotes. — [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/rippling-series-a-pitch-deck-and-memo) (via search excerpt)
- **Front's 2016 Series A deck** is reported at 19, 21, or 24 slides (sources conflict). — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/front-series-saas-startup-pitch-deck/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended targets by deck type:**
  - Presenting: 10–12 slides
  - Demo Day: 5–7 slides
  - Send-ahead: 12–20 pages, with 16 as the 2023 average and 19–20 as DocSend's upper recommendation
  - Leave-behind and data room: core plus appendix, no hard cap
- **On iPreneur's 20–30 slide target (see memory note on slide count):**
  - A 20–30-slide deck sits at or above the measured averages: 16 in 2023, about 19–20 for 2015 successful decks.
  - DocSend's 2023 data associates longer-than-average decks with unsuccessful pre-seed raises, and CRV names 40-slide decks as a mistake.
  - To keep the 20–30 target without violating the evidence, structure output as a **core narrative of about 12–18 slides plus a labeled appendix of about 6–12 slides** (financial model detail, cohort/unit economics, detailed GTM, product deep-dive, testimonials, risks, cap table/round terms).
  - Also offer a one-click "presenting cut" (10–12 slides) and a "Demo Day cut" (5–7 slides) derived from the same sections.
- Section-split slides can help: "Traction" can span two slides (growth chart plus retention or pipeline), and "Team" can span founders plus advisors. This adds slides without adding ideas, which is consistent with Hale's "one idea per slide."

### Gaps
- No study found correlates slide count with funding outcomes beyond DocSend's 2023 pre-seed finding (65% of unsuccessful decks above average length). No 2025–2026 primary DocSend slide-count average was retrievable.
- No investor survey specifically on 20–30-slide decks was found.
- The current YC Demo Day pitch length and slide count could not be confirmed from an official YC source.

## Q4. What is the optimal ordering, and how should it change with strong traction, weak traction, or a team-led pitch?

### Takeaway
The default spine is Sequoia's order: purpose → problem → solution → why now → market → competition → business model → team → financials → vision/ask. DocSend's successful decks followed it but moved product and team earlier. Every modern source says the order is flexible and the strongest proof should lead: traction first when it is strong; team, insight, or vision first when it is not.

### Cited Findings
- DocSend 2015: the most successful slide order was "similar to Sequoia Capital's recommended order, except that product and team moved earlier." — [TechCrunch 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/06/08/lessons-from-a-study-of-perfect-pitch-decks-vcs-spend-an-average-of-3-minutes-44-seconds-on-them/) **[pre-2020]**
- DocSend 2026 seed order: purpose → problem → solution → market → (why now) → product → competition → traction → team → business model → (financials) → ask. — [DocSend via excerpt](https://www.docsend.com/blog/what-vcs-really-want-to-see-inside-your-seed-deck/)
- CRV order: cover → problem → solution → product → market → business model → traction → competition → team → ask ("Order is flexible"). — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- YC (Seibel): one-liner → market → **progress (traction) third** → insight → business model → team → ask. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- Strong traction: "Sometimes open with strong traction." Justin Kan: "Don't bury the lead." — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Weak traction: "Without traction, you must rely more on your story, credentials, and vision." — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Concept pitch: Hoffman ran LinkedIn's Series B as a concept pitch because "our data at that point wasn't impressive," and he advised opening with the investment thesis. — [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/billionaire-reid-hoffman-reveals-linkedins-2004-pitch-deck/229469)
- Lead with what is most unique about the company. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)
- Team-first logic: "team quality consistently ranks as the single most important factor in early stage decisions." — [CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)
- Pre-product companies can lean on team credentials and market validation. — [Waveup (Vendor)](https://waveup.com/blog/what-to-include-in-a-pitch-deck/)
- Airbnb 2009 order (secondary): cover → problem → solution → market validation → market size → product → business model → adoption → competition → competitive advantages → team → press → testimonials → financials. — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startups-airbnb-pitch-deck)

### Inferences
Suggested ordering profiles for the generator, as a synthesis of the above:

- **Default (balanced seed):**
  1. Title/one-liner
  2. Problem
  3. Solution/insight
  4. Why now
  5. Product
  6. Market
  7. Business model
  8. Traction
  9. GTM
  10. Competition/moat
  11. Team
  12. Financials
  13. Ask/use of funds/milestones
  14. Vision
  15. Appendix
- **Traction-led** (meaningful revenue or growth): Title → headline traction metric (the "lead") → Problem → Solution → Product → Traction detail → Business model/unit economics → Market → GTM → Competition → Team → Financials → Ask → Vision.
- **Team-led or concept** (pre-product, deep-tech, repeat founders): Title → Investment thesis or vision → Team (moved to slide 2–3, with founder-problem fit) → Problem → Insight/why now → Solution/product → Market → Competition/moat → Business model → Validation (LOIs, pilots) → Milestones → Ask. Add a risks-and-mitigations slide (Hoffman).
- In all profiles, the ask and use-of-funds slides stay near the end, and the cover must already state the one-liner and, per CRV, the raise amount.

### Gaps
- No controlled data compares the success rates of traction-first and problem-first ordering. The guidance is expert opinion plus DocSend's 2015 observational finding.

## Q5. How do send-ahead decks, presenting decks, one-pagers, and memos differ?

### Takeaway
- **Send-ahead:** a standalone, text-heavier PDF built to win a meeting. No appendices.
- **Presenting deck:** a sparse visual aid (at most about 10 words per slide, 30–35+ pt text) that only works with narration.
- **Leave-behind:** a hybrid that adds the appendix.
- **Teaser or one-pager:** 3–4 slides, or 5 email bullets.
- **Memo:** prose narrative, preferred by some investors (Hustle Fund) and used successfully by Rippling. Usually paired with a metrics deck, not used alone.

### Cited Findings
- Kamps' four deck types — [TechCrunch, July 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/18/pitch-deck-contexts/):
  - **Teaser:** 3–4 slides covering team, traction, problem/solution, market size, round details. Can be wordier. Keep proprietary information out.
  - **Send-ahead:** more words than a presentation, no appendices, PDF only. Consider what a competitor could do with the data if it is forwarded.
  - **Presentation:** at most 10 words per slide, text at least 35 pt, graphics over text, no animations, builds, or video.
  - **Leave-behind:** hybrid of the two, plus appendices for Q&A. "This is the only time you send appendices!"
- The send-ahead must be self-explanatory and is often used by an associate to sell the deal internally. Sending the presentation deck in its place is a common mistake. — [Road Less Ventured (Medium)](https://medium.com/road-less-ventured/pitch-deck-series-part-7-a-tale-of-two-decks-c4809170e8f4); [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-choose-the-right-pitch-deck-variation)
- **Conflict on send-ahead length:** some practitioners say it should be shorter than the presentation deck, others say fuller, around 12–15 slides with more text. — [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-choose-the-right-pitch-deck-variation); [Road Less Ventured](https://medium.com/road-less-ventured/pitch-deck-series-part-7-a-tale-of-two-decks-c4809170e8f4)
- Presenting decks: the audience "cannot read a slide AND listen to you simultaneously," and slides should support the story, not dominate it. — [YC Blog, Ralston](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)
- Written pitches: Elizabeth Yin prefers them as "faster to process," and is format-agnostic between a Notion page and a deck. — [Mercury](https://blog.mercury.com/series-tea/elizabeth-yin)
- Email or one-pager: about five bullets (problem, solution, traction, market size, team), because investors spend 5–30 seconds on emails. — [SaaS Club](https://saasclub.io/podcast/saas-fundraising-elizabeth-yin-hustle-fund/)
- Seibel's two-sentence "Email Test" is the atomic unit of every format. — [Michael Seibel](https://www.michaelseibel.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company)
- **Memo-style:**
  - Rippling raised a $45M Series A with no formal deck. The centerpiece was an investor memo, plus 46 slides of metrics, projections, and footnotes. — [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/rippling-series-a-pitch-deck-and-memo)
  - Pro: investors end up writing an internal memo anyway, and a founder memo reveals depth of thinking. — [Phil Unleashed](https://philunleashed.substack.com/p/for-fundraising-founders-your-deck); [Melanie (Medium)](https://medium.com/@jewelmelanie/why-founders-should-write-memos-7d21a8caaffb)
  - Con: slides force brevity, and memos demand more investor time. — same search set, attributions mixed; see also [Speedrun Substack](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/your-pitch-deck-isnt-enough)
- Hoffman: "lead with the investment thesis," the same move a memo's opening paragraph makes. — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2013/10/reid-hoffman-linkedin-pitch-deck-10-pieces-advice)

### Inferences
- iPreneur's single section model can render all formats from the same content: the presenting deck uses headline-only fields, the send-ahead uses headline plus 2–3 supporting sentences, and the memo concatenates section bodies into prose. Each section should store three text lengths:
  - a 7–10-word headline
  - a 25–60-word send-ahead body
  - a 100–250-word memo paragraph
- Appendix slides should be excluded from send-ahead exports by default and included in leave-behind or data-room exports.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found specifically on Amazon 6-pager-style fundraising memos beyond Rippling's example and opinion posts.
- No data compares the success rates of memo-based and deck-based raises.

## Q6. What structural patterns do famous decks share?

### Takeaway
The publicly analyzed decks (Airbnb 2009, LinkedIn 2004 Series B, Front 2016 Series A) share a problem → solution → market → product → model → competition → team spine, concrete numbers on market and traction, and a single dominant revenue story. The Series A and B decks are longer (about 20–40 slides), metric-heavy, and candid about weaknesses or risks.

### Cited Findings
- **Airbnb 2009 seed** (secondary teardowns): 14 slides:
  1. Cover
  2. Problem: three problems, pricing, culture, and technology
  3. Solution: one sentence plus three benefits, save money, make money, share culture
  4. Market validation: CouchSurfing users
  5. Market size: 2B+ trips; capture share → 84M trips
  6. Product
  7. Business model
  8. Adoption/GTM: Craigslist cross-posting
  9. Competition: hotels, CouchSurfing, VRBO, Craigslist
  10. Competitive advantages
  11. Team
  12. Press
  13. Testimonials
  14. Financials
  — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startups-airbnb-pitch-deck); [VC Beast](https://vcbeast.com/airbnb-pitch-deck-original-2009-analysis)
- **LinkedIn 2004 Series B** (Greylock, about $10M, per secondary sources): a concept pitch led by an investment thesis, emphasizing one revenue stream. Hoffman rejected the "Friendster for business" analogy because investors wanted clear revenue, and later recommended adding risks. — [Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/business-news/billionaire-reid-hoffman-reveals-linkedins-2004-pitch-deck/229469); [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2013/10/reid-hoffman-linkedin-pitch-deck-10-pieces-advice)
- **Front 2016 Series A** (about $10M): 19–24 slides (sources conflict). Lead investor reported as Social Capital by one source and Sequoia by others (conflict). Mathilde Collin published the deck with slide-by-slide annotations. Features cited:
  - a capital-efficiency slide
  - net-retention cohorts
  - candor about gaps
  - framing a new category ("inbox for teams")
  — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/front-series-saas-startup-pitch-deck/); [MyPitchDecks](https://mypitchdecks.com/case-studies/front-pitch-deck)
- **Rippling 2019 Series A:** memo plus a 46-slide metrics appendix instead of a narrative deck. — [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/rippling-series-a-pitch-deck-and-memo)
- **YC Demo Day redesigns:** Shred Video, Afrostream, SparkGift, Meadow, Dropbox, and Magic. Before-and-after examples show shorter text, bigger type, captioned graphs, and steps replacing screenshots. — [YC Blog, Hale 2015](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-design-a-better-pitch-deck)

### Inferences
- Shared pattern: (1) a one-sentence definition, (2) a quantified problem, (3) external demand validation (CouchSurfing for Airbnb; retention cohorts for Front), (4) one revenue model, (5) named competitors, and (6) team. Later-stage decks add capital efficiency, cohort retention, and risk or candor slides. This argues for iPreneur adding a "Key metrics / unit economics" section and an optional "Risks & mitigations" section when the stage is Series A or later.
- Famous seed decks (Airbnb, 14 slides) sit near the 10–16 core range, while Series A and B decks run longer. This supports making slide count scale with stage: pre-seed/seed core of about 12–16 slides, Series A+ core of about 16–22 slides, plus appendix.

### Gaps
- **Uber (2008), YouTube (2005), Facebook (2004), Buffer (2011), Intercom:** no sources were retrieved this session. Their slide structures and counts are therefore not documented here.
- Most famous-deck details come from vendor teardowns with conflicting specifics (slide counts, user numbers, lead investors). Original decks or founder posts were not fetched.
- All famous decks analyzed predate 2020. No widely published, verified 2024–2026 successful deck was analyzed. AI-era decks, where CRV flags "undefended AI claims," are a gap.

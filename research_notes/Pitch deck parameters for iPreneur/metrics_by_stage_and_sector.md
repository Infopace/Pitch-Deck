# Pitch-Deck Metrics, Benchmarks and Thresholds by Funding Stage and Sector (2025–2026)

Research date: 27 Oct 2026. Scope: mainly US and global. Every number below gives its source, period and sample where known. Most primary report pages (Carta learn/data pages, KeyBanc PDF) returned HTTP 403 or show their charts only as images. Where a figure came from a search snippet or a secondary blog rather than from the primary page, it is marked **[secondary]** or **[unverified]**. Q3 2026 editions of Carta State of Private Markets and the PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor were not found in search, so the latest primary market data here is Q2 2026 / H1 2026.

## Q1. By stage: traction levels, round sizes, valuations, dilution, runway, and milestones the round should unlock

### Takeaway
In 2026 the median US seed is roughly $3–4.5M at about $24M post-money with ~18% dilution. The median Series A is about $15–19M at about $76–79M post-money, and the typical Series A company has around $2.5M ARR. Series B dilution has fallen to about 13%. AI companies get a large valuation premium and absorb most capital. The median gap from seed to Series A is now about 20–23 months, so a seed or Series A ask should fund at least 18–24 months of runway (many advisors now say 24–30 months).

### Cited Findings

**Market context (2026)**
- Q1 2026 (Cooley, 165 deals, $39.9B invested): up rounds were 86% of deals, flat rounds 2.6% and down rounds 11.4%, up from 79.5% / 7.3% / 13.2% in Q4 2025. 98.2% of deals had a 1x liquidation preference and 96.4% were non-participating preferred. Deal volume was the lowest since Q3 2016. 42% of deals were priced above $100M pre-money. — [Cooley Q1 2026 Venture Financing Report](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-04-29-q1-2026-venture-financing-report)
- Megadeals of $100M or more were reportedly 87.5% of the $412.7B deployed in H1 2026, mostly in AI. **[secondary, attributed to PitchBook-NVCA]** — [ValueAddVC, updated Oct 2026](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)
- AI companies took 49% of pre-seed dollars in H1 2026 (Carta). — [Carta State of Pre-Seed Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2026/) (snippet only; page returned 403)

**Pre-seed**

Of post-money SAFEs in H1 2026, 94% had a valuation cap: cap only in 73%, cap plus discount in 21%. (Carta data, as tabulated by Waveup.) — [Waveup seed benchmarks](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/); [Carta SAFE caps Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/safe-valuation-caps-q2-2026/)
- Average pre-seed instrument (single SAFE, not a whole round) was $276K, up 27% year on year (Carta, Q2 2026). — [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- Median post-money SAFE caps for 2025 were about $10M for rounds of $250K–$1M and about $15M for rounds of $1M–$2.5M. For SAFEs above $2.5M, the Q2 2026 median cap was $35M (up 40% year on year), the 75th percentile was near $60M and the 90th about $100M. — [Carta State of Pre-Seed 2025](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-2025-full-report/); [Carta SAFE caps Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/safe-valuation-caps-q2-2026/) (search snippets; pages returned 403); [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- Median pre-seed pre-money was $7.7M at end Q3 2025, against $8.0M in Q2 2025. **[secondary]** — [Zeni, pre-seed valuations](https://www.zeni.ai/blog/pre-seed-valuations)
- Typical pre-seed round is about $1M (range $750K–$1.5M) at a $4–6M cap with 15–20% dilution. **[secondary, loosely sourced; this cap range conflicts with Carta's $10–15M median caps above]** — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)
- Pre-seed traction bar: "a functional minimum viable product (MVP) or strong early validation clears the bar". SAFEs are typical and median dilution is in the mid-to-high teens. — [CRV, Startup Traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction)

- Carta's learn page reportedly gives Q2 2026 seed medians of $4.5M raised at $23.9M post-money. **[unverified: search snippet only, page returned 403]** — [Carta, Seed Funding](https://carta.com/learn/startups/fundraising/seed-funding/)

- Median seed post-money across all sectors (Carta): $16M in Q4 2023, $18M in Q4 2024 and $24M in Q4 2025. Top-decile seed valuations are nearly 4x the median (Q1 2026). — [Waveup citing Carta](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- **AI premium:** AI seed pre-money was $19M against $13M for non-AI (Carta data shared by Peter Walker, early 2025). Carta's 2025 year-end data put the AI Series A premium at about 38%. **[secondary]** — [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/); [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/ai-startup-funding-this-quarter)

- Median employee option pool at seed is 12.1% (Carta Founder Ownership Report 2026). Founders hold about 56% fully diluted after seed and about 36% after Series A (Carta, rounds 2021–2025). Median dilution from seed through Series C fell from about 19% to 18% to 16% (2025). — [Waveup citing Carta](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)
- Seed traction bar, conflicting:
  - CRV: "$500K to $1.5M ARR for B2B SaaS", with growth rate weighted as heavily as ARR. Sustained double-digit monthly growth is "solid" and low single-digit monthly growth is "a question mark". Pre-product and pre-revenue companies "should raise pre seed instead". — [CRV, Startup Traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction)
  - ValueAddVC: "$50K–$200K ARR, strong week-1 retention, or an enterprise pilot" (uncited). — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)

**Seed → Series A gap**
- The median gap from seed to Series A was 616 days (about 20 months) in Q2 2025 and 1.9 years in Q4 2025 (Carta, reported February 2026). — [Waveup citing Carta](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/); [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)
- Seed → Series A conversion was about 38% for Carta's 2020–2024 cohorts, down from 50%+ in 2020–21. Pre-seed → seed was about 45% within 24 months and Series A → B about 55%. **[secondary, attributed to Carta]** — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)
- Time between rounds in 2026 is quoted as 18–24 months for seed→A (against 12–14 months in 2021) and 22–28 months for A→B (against 15–18 months in 2021). **[uncited]** — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)
- Runway: CRV's FAQ suggests 24–30 months of seed runway (uncited). Waveup advises budgeting 18–24 months from seed to Series A. — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators); [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)

**Series A**
- Round size and valuation, conflicting:
  - Carta learn page: $14.7M median round at $76.3M post-money (Q2 2026). **[unverified: snippet only]** — [Carta, Series A](https://carta.com/learn/startups/fundraising/series-a/)
  - ValueAddVC citing PitchBook-NVCA Q2 2026: $19.4M median round and $78.7M+ post-money. Non-AI Series A rounds are reportedly still pricing at about $40–42M pre-money. — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)

**Series B**
- Median dilution fell from about 15% to **12.9% in 2025**, the largest drop of any stage (Carta). — [Carta State of Private Markets: 2025 in Review](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q4-2025/) (snippet; page returned 403)
- Median pre-money was **$118.9M for primary rounds** and $142.4M for bridge rounds in Q3 2025 (Carta). **[secondary]** — [Zeni, Series B valuations](https://www.zeni.ai/blog/series-b-valuations)
- Cooley's sample shows a higher Series B median pre-money: $195M in Q4 2025 and **$165M in Q1 2026**. Cooley's deal mix skews larger, so it does not match Carta. — [Cooley Q1 2026](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-04-29-q1-2026-venture-financing-report)
- Round size and traction **[uncited]**: median round about $40M (against $35M previously) at $120–160M+ post-money. A "clean B" is $5M+ ARR growing 100%+ YoY, NRR above 115%, CAC payback under 18 months and a clear path to the Rule of 40; the typical B is $5–10M ARR at 80–120% YoY. Bridge or down-round triggers are ARR below $3M on a 2022-era valuation, growth below 80%, churn above 15% and a burn multiple above 2.0x. — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/startup-funding-rounds-in-2025-whats-normal-at-pre-seed-seed-a-and-b)

- AI and non-AI should be benchmarked separately for valuation. Blended medians are pulled up by AI.

## Q2. Core SaaS/B2B metrics and benchmark values

### Takeaway
The private B2B SaaS medians for 2025 are modest: NRR about 101–102%, GRR about 88–90%, ARR growth about 20% across all sizes, CAC payback about 16–24 months, Rule of 40 about 25%, and LTV:CAC about 3–4x. A venture-backed company raising a Series A needs top-quartile values, not medians: about 2–3x growth, NRR above 110–120%, burn multiple under about 1.5x, and payback under about 18 months.

### Cited Findings

**Growth (T2D3 and its variants)**
- T2D3 means triple, triple, double, double, double. Bessemer's AI-era "Q2T3" means quadruple, quadruple, triple, triple, triple, based on less than five years of data. — [Bessemer State of AI 2025](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-state-of-ai-2025)
- Bessemer *Scaling to $100M* (2024 update): average growth for companies with $1–10M ARR is "nearly 200%", and the middle 50% grow 100–230%. Above $50M ARR, average growth is 60% and the middle 50% grow 35–80%. Growth endurance (this year's growth divided by last year's) is GOOD at 70%, BETTER at 75% and BEST at 80%. Time from $1M to $100M ARR is 10 years (good), 7 years (better) or 5 years (best). — [Bessemer, Scaling to $100M (PDF)](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2021/09/Scaling-to-100-Million-by-Mary-DOnofrio_updated2024.pdf)
- KeyBanc/Sapphire 2025 (16th survey, released 13 Nov 2025) projected median private SaaS ARR growth rising from 15% in 2024 to 20% in 2025, the first acceleration in three years. — [Sapphire Ventures summary](https://info.sapphireventures.com/2025-keybanc-capital-markets-sapphire-ventures-saas-survey); [Press release](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/36109511/private-saas-company-survey-reveals-ai-driven-transformation-and-sustained-operational-excellence)

**Net and gross revenue retention**
- SaaS Capital 2025 (companies with more than $1M ARR), ACV band $25–50K: median NRR **102%**, top quartile 111%, bottom quartile 97%. Companies with the highest ACVs have the highest gross retention. Other bands appear only in charts. — [SaaS Capital retention benchmarks 2025](https://www.saas-capital.com/research/saas-retention-benchmarks-for-private-b2b-companies/)
- KeyBanc/Sapphire 2025: gross retention "near 90%" and net retention "above 100%", with AI-native and AI-enabled companies leading. — [Sapphire](https://info.sapphireventures.com/2025-keybanc-capital-markets-sapphire-ventures-saas-survey)
- A re-analysis of KeyBanc editions shows median NDR falling from a 109% peak in 2021 to 101% in 2024. **[secondary]** — [CFO Success Coaching](https://cfo.successcoaching.co/)
- Benchmarkit 2025: median B2B SaaS NRR 102% (n=230) and median LTV:CAC **4.1x**. **[secondary]** — [Data-Mania B2B SaaS Benchmarks 2026](https://www.data-mania.com/blog/b2b-saas-benchmarks-2026-annual-report/)
- Median private B2B SaaS GRR is in the "high-80s to low-90s", and stronger companies reach the mid-to-high 90s. — [CRV KPIs 2026](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)

**CAC payback**
- Estimates conflict:
  - Benchmarkit 2025 median is reported as 16 months (down from 18 months) in one secondary table and 20 months in another. — [ScaleXP](https://www.scalexp.com/blog/saas-benchmarks-cac-payback/); [Data-Mania](https://www.data-mania.com/blog/b2b-saas-benchmarks-2026-annual-report/)
  - CRV says "typically around 20 months for private SaaS". — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)
  - A KeyBanc re-analysis gives fully-loaded payback of about 24 months, and new-customer-only payback of 37 months by 2024. It also notes that the report's printed formula is wrong. — [CFO Success Coaching](https://cfo.successcoaching.co/)
- Bessemer's CAC payback formula: last quarter's S&M ÷ (new CMRR added last quarter × gross margin %). — [Bessemer, Scaling to $100M](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2021/09/Scaling-to-100-Million-by-Mary-DOnofrio_updated2024.pdf)

**Burn multiple**
- Definition: net burn ÷ net new ARR (David Sacks, 23 Apr 2020). The post's text calls 3x+ a sign that growth "required extraordinary investment", 2x "reasonable" for early stage and 5x "terrible". — [Sacks, The Burn Multiple](https://sacks.substack.com/p/the-burn-multiple-51a7e43cb200)
- Threshold table cited by CRV (TechCrunch framework, 15 Jan 2024): **<1.0x exceptional; 1.0–1.5x strong; 1.5–2.0x solid; 2.0–3.0x concerning; >3.0x red flag**. Series A companies are expected to be below 2.0x and the strongest are near 1.0x. Companies under $1M ARR often run above 2.0x. — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)
- Bessemer's older rule for companies under $30M ARR is net new ARR ÷ net burn > 1, which is equivalent to a burn multiple under 1x. Above $30M it uses an efficiency score (% ARR growth + % burn) with targets of 70% at $30–60M ARR, 50% around $100M and 30% above $150M. **[2017 vintage]** — [Bessemer, Scaling to $100M](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2021/09/Scaling-to-100-Million-by-Mary-DOnofrio_updated2024.pdf)

**Rule of 40 and magic number**
- Aleph × Benchmarkit 2026 report (n=342): 2025 median Rule of 40 was **25%**, top quartile above 43%, up 10 points year on year. — [Aleph](https://getaleph.com/answers/rule-of-40-saas-2026)
- HighAlpha, citing a 2025 SaaS benchmarks report: Rule of 40 medians fall from 33% at $1–5M ARR to 20% at $5–20M ARR. Several sources say the metric means little below about $20M ARR. — [HighAlpha](https://www.highalpha.com/blog/mastering-the-saas-tightrope-between-growth-efficiency-and-ai-costs-in-2026); [Aleph](https://getaleph.com/answers/rule-of-40-saas-2026)
- Magic number: no reliable 2025 median. Secondary roundups conflict: 1.37 (up from 0.94) in Aleph × Benchmarkit 2026, under 0.6 for early and mid stage, and about 0.9 in growth bands. A common heuristic is that below 0.75 growth is hard to sustain and below 0.5 is a red flag. **[vendor heuristic]** — [search results summarised; e.g. Mowt benchmarks](https://mowt.pages.dev/benchmarks)

**LTV:CAC, revenue per employee, ACV and sales cycle**
- An LTV:CAC floor of 3:1 is standard, and top performers are well above it. — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)
- SaaS Capital 2025: median revenue per employee for private SaaS is about $129.7K. **[secondary snippet]** — [SaaS Capital Efficiency Metrics guide](https://www.saasmag.com/saas-capital-efficiency-metrics/)

### Inferences
- **Proposed SaaS metric reference table** (values for an early-stage, venture-backed company; medians are all-private-SaaS 2025):

| Metric | Private median (2025) | "Good" for a Series A/B raise | Red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARR growth YoY | ~20% (all sizes) | $1–10M ARR: 100–230% (Bessemer middle 50%); T2D3 / Q2T3 for AI | <100% at Series A; <80% at Series B |
| NRR | ~101–102% | ≥110% (A), ≥115–120% (B); top quartile 111%+ | <100% |
| GRR | ~88–90% | ≥90% (SMB ≥80–85% acceptable); mid-to-high 90s for enterprise | <80% |
| Gross margin (SaaS) | ~70%+ (traditional) | 70–80%+; AI products see Q3 | <50% without a credible path up |
| CAC payback | ~16–24 months | <12–18 months (SMB), <24 months (enterprise) | >24–36 months |
| LTV:CAC | 3–4x | ≥3x | <2x |
| Burn multiple | n/a | <1.5x (≤1x exceptional) | >2–3x |
| Rule of 40 | ~25% | ≥40% (relevant above ~$20M ARR) | <0–10% at scale |
| Magic number | disputed | ≥0.75 | <0.5 |
| Growth endurance | n/a | ≥70–80% | <60% |

- The generator should ask for **raw drivers** (MRR by month, new / expansion / churned MRR, S&M spend, gross margin, net burn, headcount) and compute these metrics itself. Self-reported ratios are definition-sensitive.
- For logo churn, ACV and sales cycle, give ranges by segment (SMB, mid-market, enterprise) rather than a single number. No authoritative 2025 numeric benchmark was found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–2026 data was retrievable for logo churn, ACV distribution or sales-cycle length by segment. The KeyBanc/Sapphire full report (gated) and the SaaS Capital charts (images only) were not readable.
- The OpenView benchmarks are archived and not updated after 2023. ICONIQ "Topline Growth & Operational Efficiency" was not retrieved.
- The magic number median is unreliable across sources.

## Q3. How investors evaluate AI-native startups in 2025–2026

### Takeaway
Investors judge AI companies on four things: growth speed (Q2T3 rather than T2D3), revenue quality (contracted, renewing ARR as opposed to pilot or usage run-rate), gross-margin trajectory (AI product margins averaged about 45% in 2025 against 70–80% for classic SaaS), and capital and people efficiency (ARR per FTE). Defensibility and retention matter more now that early hypergrowth "means less now than ever before".

### Cited Findings
- **Bessemer State of AI 2025** has two cohorts:
  - "Supernovas" (about 10 companies): about $40M ARR in year 1 and $125M in year 2, gross margin around 25% and "often negative", **$1.13M ARR per FTE** (4–5x typical SaaS), and "fragile" retention.
  - "Shooting Stars": ARR of about $3M in year 1, $12M in year 2, $40M in year 3 and $103M in year 4 (the Q2T3 path), gross margin about **60%**, and about **$164K ARR per FTE** in early years.
  - Bessemer calls Shooting Stars "the most important benchmark for AI founders to aim for". It says "Early hypergrowth alone means less now than ever before" and that sustainable growth depends on retention, engagement and capital efficiency. Promising AI categories draw 2–3x as many rivals as in prior years.
  - — [Bessemer State of AI 2025](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-state-of-ai-2025)
- **ICONIQ 2026 State of AI** (July 2026; about 300 executives at software companies building AI products; Q2 2026 survey; 87% North America):
  - AI product gross margin was **45% in 2025**, projected at 53% for 2026 and 59% for 2027.
  - Consumption-based pricing rose from 35% to 42% and outcome-based pricing from 18% to 23% over six months. Companies blend 1.7 pricing models on average and use about 3.3 models each.
  - AI products were 32% of revenue in 2025, projected at 42% in 2026 and about 53% in 2027.
  - Two-thirds report improved per-query unit economics. Model inference costs rise from beta to GA.
  - — [ICONIQ State of AI 2026](https://www.iconiq.com/growth/reports/state-of-ai-2026)
- The ICONIQ figures are executive projections. The sample includes AI features added to existing SaaS, not only AI-native firms. The 2026 projection moved from 52% (January 2026 snapshot) to 53% (July). — [Spendline](https://www.spendline.ai/guides/ai-gross-margin-benchmarks/); [ICONIQ Jan 2026 snapshot](https://www.iconiq.com/growth/reports/2026-state-of-ai-bi-annual-snapshot)
- SaaStr's read of ICONIQ 2026: ARR per FTE at high-growth companies is $270K (2025), projected to reach $496K (2027). Projected 2027 gross margin is 64% for high-growth companies against 58% for others, and 67% for AI infrastructure and platform against 60% for application layer. — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-builders-economy-10-metrics-from-iconiqs-newest-2026-state-of-ai-report/)
- Inference is about 20–23% of total AI product costs (ICONIQ 2025, cited by CRV). Spendline instead frames 23% as a share of revenue at scaling-stage companies. These bases differ. — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators); [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin/)
- Directional 2026 gross margin ranges: AI-augmented SaaS about 80%, AI-enabled 60–79%, AI-native 50–59%. **[vendor, not traced to primary]** — [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/ai-gross-margin/)
- **ARR quality scrutiny (2026):**
  - Investors are pushing back on AI ARR claims because pilots and short trials often don't convert to production, which makes monthly receipts volatile (April 2026). — [LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ai-startups-arr-metrics-draw-growing-scrutiny-9cf8e79e)
  - Some AI companies use looser ARR definitions, and "the real test is whether those headline numbers convert into durable, renewing revenue" (June 2026). — [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/ai-startups-are-making-arr-harder-to-trust/)
  - Example of an "ARR" that was an annualised extrapolation of recent monthly usage. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/revenue-retention-and-supernova-growth-how-vcs-judge-ai-start-ups)
  - Investors check customer concentration, contract structure, renewal versus new-logo revenue and expansion. AI NRR benchmarks "are not well established". — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)
- One Series A guide argues that "$2M in one-time consulting engagements vs $2M in recurring API fees" is the difference between a seed extension and Series A conviction. — [Angel Investors Network, AI Series A](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/venture-capital/series-a-funding-requirements-for-ai-startups-2026)
- About 64.3% of US VC deal value went to AI/ML in the quarter covered by PitchBook-NVCA's 2025 Venture Monitor (cited by CRV via Inc.). — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)

### Inferences
- **AI-specific fields the generator should request and display:**
  - Revenue split: contracted recurring, usage or consumption run-rate, pilots/POCs and services. Label each, and never sum pilots into ARR.
  - Gross margin now and its trajectory, with inference cost as a % of revenue. About 50–60% is "normal" for AI-native today; show the path to 60–70%+.
  - ARR per FTE (Shooting Star about $164K early; high-growth about $270K; Supernova about $1M+).
  - Growth versus Q2T3, plus cohort retention or NRR with usage cohorts.
  - Pricing model (seat, usage, outcome).
  - Model dependency (number of model providers and switching ability) and the defensibility story: proprietary data or feedback loops, workflow embedding, distribution, integration depth.
- An AI deck should state its ARR definition explicitly ("committed annual contract value" against "trailing-3-month run-rate × 4"), because this is now a diligence flashpoint.

### Gaps
- No quantitative benchmark was found for "data moat" or defensibility. It remains qualitative.
- No sourced AI-specific NRR or GRR benchmark. CRV explicitly says these are not established.
- a16z's AI-specific metrics posts were not retrieved.

## Q4. Sector-specific KPIs and benchmarks

### Takeaway
Each sector has its own "traction" unit:
- **Fintech:** TPV, net take rate and loss or fraud rates by vintage, plus licences and banking partners.
- **Marketplaces:** GMV, take rate, liquidity and cohort repeat.
- **Consumer / D2C:** contribution margin after marketing, LTV:CAC, repeat rate and 6-month retention.
- **Deeptech and climate:** TRL, IP, grants and FOAK path.
- **Healthtech:** regulatory, reimbursement and clinical evidence.
- **Enterprise:** pilots that convert to contracts, and named logos.

Benchmark numbers outside SaaS are mostly from practitioner or vendor sources and should be presented as ranges.

### Cited Findings

**Fintech**
- Net take rate (revenue minus interchange, network and sponsor-bank fees, divided by volume) is what investors focus on. TPV measures scale, not profit. Fintechs are valued on net take rate, contribution margin, CAC payback and loss rates **by vintage** rather than gross volume. — [Mostly Metrics](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/the-top-metrics-for-fintech-companies); [MuffinPay](https://insights.muffinpay.com/how-investors-evaluate-fintech-startups/)
- Vendor benchmarks **[vendor estimates]** — [DDR Fintech Series A guide](https://ddr.bio/due-diligence/fintech-startup/series-a-stage):
  - TPV: $1M+ per month at seed and $10M+ per month at Series A.
  - Take rate: 0.1–0.5% for payments, 1–5% for lending and 2–10% for wealth.
  - Fraud: under 0.1% of TPV for payments and under 0.5% for lending.
- Fintech pre-seed and seed median rose 46.2% to $4M, and early-stage median rose 6.4% to $8M (PitchBook, Q1 2026, fintech only). — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/04/275455-fintech-vc-funding-remains-steady-with-record-valuations-and-bets-on-ai-and-on-chain-initiatives-report-reveals/)
- Competitive pressure in payments consistently drives take rates down over time. — [409A Valuation, fintech](https://409a-valuation.com/insights/409a-valuation-fintech-startups)

**Marketplaces**
- Take rate "usually varies from a low single-digit percent to the mid-30s", depending on fragmentation, substitutes and value-add. Managed marketplaces run higher. Liquidity is measured by fill or match rate, market depth and time to match. Fragmented supply and demand is preferred. Disintermediation (leakage) "stymies growth and suppresses monetization". (Li Jin, a16z, 18 Feb 2020.) — [a16z Marketplace Glossary](https://a16z.com/the-marketplace-glossary/)
- a16z's *13 Metrics for Marketplace Companies* (Feb 2020) is the standard checklist. It covers liquidity (match rate, depth, time to match), user and core-action cohort retention, and whether newer cohorts retain better than older ones. Calling GMV "revenue" overstates the business; revenue is GMV × take rate. — [Rework summary](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/marketplace-growth/marketplace-metrics); [Lenny's, marketplace metrics](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-most-important-marketplace-metrics)
- Indian-market guidance: a repeat transaction rate above 60% is a strong signal, and take rates above 25% create disintermediation risk. **[India blog, not a16z]** — [CFO Matrix](https://cfomatrix.in/insight/unit-economics/marketplace-unit-economics-15-metrics)

**Consumer / D2C**
- 6-month user retention, GOOD / GREAT (Lenny Rachitsky, 9 Jun 2020) — [Lenny's, What is good retention](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29):
  - Consumer social: ~25% / ~45%
  - Consumer transactional: ~30% / ~50%
  - Consumer SaaS: ~40% / ~70%
  - SMB/mid-market SaaS: ~60% / ~80%
  - Enterprise SaaS: ~70–75% / ~90%
- D2C contribution margin, median about 22% in 2025 against about 35% in 2021 as paid acquisition got more expensive. Another source puts the median at about 25% and the top quartile at about 35% (Finaloop 2024 data). An operator analysis gives 15–20% after shipping, fees, returns and ads. **[vendor/operator]** — [Commerce Catalyst](https://commercecatalyst.ai/benchmarks/); [LevelCFO](https://levelcfo.com/benchmarks/ecommerce/); [Fairview](https://getfairview.com/d2c-metrics)
- D2C LTV:CAC rule of thumb is ≥3:1, with a reported 2026 median of 3.4x. CAC rose an estimated 40–60% from 2023 to 2025. Repeat purchase rate is about 18–19% overall and 25–40% for consumables. Annual retention is about 50–60% for supplements and about 25–30% for apparel. **[vendor]** — [Commerce Catalyst](https://commercecatalyst.ai/benchmarks/); [Daymark](https://www.usedaymark.io/blog/d2c-ecommerce-benchmarks); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/customer-lifetime-value-benchmarks-2026-industry-data)
- For consumer, DAU and WAU trends matter more than total user counts. — [CRV KPIs](https://www.crv.com/content/key-performance-indicators)

**Deeptech, hardware and climate**
- TRL by stage: seed investors are comfortable at about TRL 2–3, Series A investors look for about TRL 5, and later-stage investors want proven unit economics at about TRL 9. Some funds (for example Norrsken) start at TRL 5. **[survey cited in a 2024 Climate Hardware Playbook discussion]** — [Impact VC Substack](https://impactvc.substack.com/p/investing-in-hardware-interview-with)
- TRL alone does not capture durability or commercial readiness. Some funds pair it with physical-scale metrics and capital needs. — [Extantia, Medium](https://medium.com/extantia-capital/the-traditional-funding-cycle-doesnt-work-for-climate-tech-144f88fabd24)
- Grants are non-dilutive and add credibility, and are a common way to reach a working prototype. Investors prefer to fund scaling once the core technology is proven rather than open-ended R&D. Later capital shifts to equipment leasing, project finance and revenue-based financing. — [Enduring Planet](https://enduringplanet.com/insights/preparing-to-pitch-vcs-as-a-climate-hardware-startup); [Extantia CAPEX guide](https://medium.com/extantia-capital/buckle-up-its-time-to-scale-a-capex-financing-guide-for-hardware-climate-tech-companies-c6a404c1da7); [CapStackCompass](https://thirdsphere.substack.com/p/more-funding-options-less-dilution)
- Climate hardware (direct air capture, hydrogen, batteries) typically runs 40–60% gross margins at scale. **[unsourced estimate]** — [Sustainable Atlas](https://sustainableatlas.org/post/trend-watch-venture-go-to-market-for-frontier-tech-in-2026-signals-winners-and-red-flags-1268)
- Climate hardware founders should present signed contracts with money attached rather than LOIs as evidence of product-market fit. — [Tech Nation](https://technation.io/how-to-scale-climate-tech-hardware-startup/)
- A FOAK-project Series B (about $50M) is a hard raise, and some investors suggest 2–3x return expectations for climate hardware rather than 10x. — [TechCrunch, Apr 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/27/climate-startup-valley-of-death/)

**Healthtech / digital health**
- Rock Health H1 2026 (US digital health): $7.4B across 244 deals, **median deal $14M** (up from $12M in 2025). Mega-deals of $100M+ took 45% of capital. — [Healthcare Dive](https://healthcaredive.com/news/digital-health-funding-first-half-2026-rock-health/825215/); [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/healthcare/rock-health-74b-went-into-digital-health-in-h1-2026-and-20-rounds-took-45)
- CB Insights (global, 2026 YTD): median digital-health deal $7.7M, early stage $4.8M, late stage $50M. This is not comparable with Rock Health's US-only data. — [search summary of CB Insights coverage]; see [eMarketer Q1 2026](https://www.emarketer.com/content/mega-rounds-power-q1-2026-health-tech-fundraising)
- Digital health funds underwrite regulatory, reimbursement and clinical risk together. They ask about the FDA pathway and CPT code strategy, "who pays", and the evidence that unlocks each buyer. Peer-reviewed outcomes data and regulatory traction are now baseline. Pre-FDA or pre-evidence companies should state the timeline and budget to get there. Health-system sales take 12–18 months, and contracted revenue or LOIs beat projections. — [VC Sheet, digital health funds](https://vcsheet.com/sheet/digital-health-funds)

**Enterprise**
- Strong enterprise signals are co-selling agreements or named enterprise pilots that put another company's budget behind the product. Series A diligence checks that ARR ties to renewable contracts. — [CRV Traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction)
- An LOI "signed 2 months ago on track to be converted into a contract in the next 1–3 months" is far stronger than one signed 12+ months ago. Stalled pilots weaken the case. — [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-how-to-leverage-customer-traction-to-attract-angel-investors)

### Inferences
- **Proposed sector KPI matrix:**

| Sector | Must-ask KPIs | Display as "good" (directional) |
|---|---|---|
| Fintech | TPV, net take rate, contribution margin per transaction or user, loss/fraud by vintage, licences or sponsor bank, CAC payback | TPV growth month on month; payments net take ~0.1–0.5%, lending 1–5%; fraud <0.1% TPV (payments) |
| Marketplace | GMV, take rate, net revenue, liquidity (match rate, time to match), repeat rate / cohort GMV retention, supply and demand concentration | Take rate fits category (single digits to ~30%); newer cohorts retain better; low concentration |
| Consumer app | DAU/MAU, D1/D7/D30 and M6 retention, organic share, CAC by channel | M6 retention ≥25–30% (social/transactional), ≥40% (consumer subscription) |
| D2C | AOV, CAC, contribution margin after marketing, repeat rate, LTV:CAC, payback | CM ≥25–35%; LTV:CAC ≥3x; repeat rate ≥25–40% (consumables) |
| Deeptech/hardware | TRL, IP/patents, grants won, BOM cost now against at scale, pilot or offtake contracts, capex path | TRL 2–3 at seed, ~5 at Series A; gross margin 40–60% at scale |
| Climate | As deeptech plus FOAK plan, offtakes, project-finance readiness, cost curve (for example $/ton) | Signed offtakes rather than LOIs; blended non-dilutive capital |
| Healthtech | Regulatory pathway (510(k), De Novo, PMA, none), reimbursement (CPT), clinical evidence, payer or provider contracts, sales cycle | Peer-reviewed outcomes; contracts rather than pilots; median US deal ~$14M (H1 2026) |
| Enterprise | Pilots → paid conversion rate, ACV, pipeline coverage, named logos, sales cycle | Recent LOIs converting within 1–3 months; paid pilots; referenceable logos |

### Gaps
- **Biotech clinical milestones** (Phase I/II readouts, IND, typical raise per phase) were not found in this pass.
- **No sourced numeric benchmarks** for marketplace liquidity (match rate or time-to-match targets), fintech loss rates by product, consumer DAU/MAU from an investor source, hardware unit economics, enterprise pilot-to-paid conversion or pipeline coverage.
- Lux, DCVC and Battery sector posts were not retrieved.
- Most non-SaaS numbers are vendor estimates.

## Q5. How financial projections should be presented

### Takeaway
Show a driver-based, bottom-up model: 3 years at seed and 3–5 years at Series A and later. Make the key assumptions explicit (conversion, pricing, churn or retention, CAC, hiring). A believable model beats a hockey stick, and investors discount aggressive curves automatically.

### Cited Findings
- **Horizon:** typically 3 years at seed and 5 years at Series A and later. "Most investors will want to see a three-year projection at a minimum, but five years provides for a more reasonable ramp up." — [Deckary, financial projections slide](https://deckary.com/blog/financial-projections-slide); [LaunchRock](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/pitch-deck-financial-projections)
- **Bottom-up drivers:**
  - Build from customers, price, churn and cost per acquisition, not a top-down share of a big market. "A clean, believable model with modest numbers beats a hockey-stick you can't explain." — [eChai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/startup-finance/how-do-i-build-a-simple-financial-model-for-a-pitch-deck-without-pretending-i-can-predict-revenue-three-years-out)
  - Investors "discount them automatically" when founders draw aggressive curves. Use customers × contract value × retention. — [TechCrunch, Apr 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/10/build-a-bottom-up-financial-model-to-show-potential-investors-youre-serious)
- **Assumptions investors scrutinise:**
  - Whether margins are real or subsidised burn, and the credibility of conversion and retention rates.
  - The core test is "what specifically would have to be true for this trajectory?"
  - — [LaunchRock](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/financial-projections); [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/financial-projections-slide)
- A legacy rule of thumb ($50M revenue by year 3, $100M by year 5) appears in an old Entrepreneur article. It is not a current standard, though the article also notes investors dislike "slow and solid". **[dated]** — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/159516)

### Inferences
- The generator should collect the drivers (starting customers or MRR, new customers per month by channel, ACV or ARPU, churn, expansion, gross margin, headcount plan, CAC by channel) and compute a 3-year view (seed) or 3–5-year view (A+). Show base and downside cases.
- Run a sanity check against Q2: implied growth versus T2D3 or Q2T3, payback, and burn multiple. Flag projections that imply a growth rate or efficiency well above top-quartile benchmarks.
- Projections should connect to the ask: show cash balance and runway month by month, and when the next-round milestones are hit.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 primary investor survey quantifies how heavily projections are discounted or which horizon VCs prefer.

## Q6. How the "ask" slide should be structured

### Takeaway
The ask should state the amount, the instrument (a post-money SAFE at pre-seed or seed, priced preferred at Series A and later), a use-of-funds breakdown tied to dated milestones, and the runway it buys (18–24+ months). The milestones should be the next round's bar.

### Cited Findings
- **Instrument:** at pre-seed, 93% of rounds are SAFEs, 91% of SAFEs are post-money, and 94% of post-money SAFEs carry a cap (Carta, H1/Q2 2026). Most early rounds under $4M in 2025 used SAFEs or convertible notes. Priced rounds at Series A and later are almost all 1x non-participating preferred (Cooley Q1 2026: 98.2% at 1x, 96.4% non-participating). — [Waveup citing Carta](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/); [Carta SAFE caps](https://carta.com/data/safe-valuation-caps-q2-2026/); [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-04-29-q1-2026-venture-financing-report)
- **Runway:** 18–24 months gives "roughly 12–18 months to execute and hit your milestones before starting the next fundraise". Some advise raising for 24 months to build in a cushion. — [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-make-the-ask-in-a-pitch-deck); [PitchDeckGuide](https://pitchdeckguide.com/pitch-deck-ask-slide/)
- **Use of funds:**
  - Practitioner examples split about 70% product/engineering, 20% go-to-market and 10% G&A, and advise adding a 20–25% buffer. They warn that "Product, marketing, ops" with three round percentages "isn't a use of funds". **[examples, not benchmarks]** — [Waveup, ask and use of funds](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-craft-a-winning-ask-and-use-of-funds-slide-with-examples/); [VentureMage](https://venturemage.com/pitch-deck-ask-slide)
  - Tie each spending bucket to a dated outcome (for example "$1M ARR by Q4 2026"). Vague milestones such as "market leadership" signal a lack of operating maturity. — [PitchDeckGuide](https://pitchdeckguide.com/pitch-deck-ask-slide/); [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-craft-a-winning-ask-and-use-of-funds-slide-with-examples/)
- **Disagreement on stating terms:** some guides show wording such as "$1.5M on $8M post-money SAFE", while others say "never put a valuation or cap in the deck you email to investors". — [StartupFundraising.com](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/how-to-make-the-ask-in-a-pitch-deck); [PitchDeckGuide](https://pitchdeckguide.com/pitch-deck-ask-slide/)
- **Seed-to-A timing:** the median gap of about 20 months (Q2 2025) to 1.9 years (Q4 2025) implies a seed should fund at least about 24 months. — [Waveup citing Carta](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- **Ask slide template for the generator:**
  1. Amount (and minimum close if relevant).
  2. Instrument (post-money SAFE, priced preferred or convertible note), with the cap or valuation shown optionally behind a toggle, because guidance conflicts.
  3. Use of funds in 3–5 specific buckets (people by role, GTM programmes, infrastructure or compute, regulatory or capex where relevant), each with % and $.
  4. Runway in months at the planned burn.
  5. 3–4 dated milestones that equal the next round's bar from Q1.
  6. Optional: committed or soft-circled amounts and lead investor status.
- Sector-specific use-of-funds lines:
  - AI: compute and inference.
  - Hardware: tooling, BOM and certification.
  - Healthtech: clinical studies and regulatory submissions.
  - Fintech: licences, compliance and loss reserves.

### Gaps
- No data source gives actual median use-of-funds percentages by stage. The splits found are illustrative examples.
- No primary data on how often decks disclose caps or valuations.

## Q7. Pre-revenue substitutes for traction

### Takeaway
Committed demand beats expressed interest. In descending order of weight: dollar-denominated LOIs with named counterparties, paid pilots with a conversion path, active design partners (data access and weekly use), pre-orders, co-selling agreements, then qualified waitlists. Team shipping velocity and, for developer tools, OSS and community pull also count.

### Cited Findings
- **CRV's list** — [CRV, Startup Traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction):
  - LOIs are strongest "when they carry dollar figures".
  - Design partners should be "testing weekly builds".
  - Pilot commitments and pre-orders count, and are stronger when the customer stays engaged.
  - Partnerships count when they are co-selling or named enterprise pilots.
  - A waitlist from "one tweet shows little". A strong case names the buyer, the use case and what was signed or paid.
  - Team momentum: how much shipped relative to time spent on the problem.
  - For developer tools: OSS pull, active communities and repeat developer usage.
- "30 of the right buyers outweigh 3,000 random emails". Use "10 LOIs from [logos]", not "lots of interest". — [Flowjam, pre-seed traction](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/how-to-show-traction-for-pre-seed-startup-2-ve541)
- LOI recency and conversion matter. A recent LOI converting within 1–3 months is a much stronger signal than an old one, and stalled pilots hurt. — [Hustle Fund](https://www.hustlefund.vc/post/angel-squad-how-to-leverage-customer-traction-to-attract-angel-investors)
- A common pre-seed profile is "a couple of pilots that are agreed upon or even paid but not retained", and a paid pilot is typical before seed. Design-partner example: "5 named companies who've committed internal champions plus data access once v1 is ready". — [Haley Bryant, Revenue quality at pre-seed](https://haleymbryant.substack.com/p/revenue-quality-at-pre-seed); [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/raise-seed-funding-pre-product-pre-revenue)
- Pre-product, investors underwrite "Can this team actually pull this off?" Some seeds close with zero revenue on compelling growth and engagement. One source cites 50+ customer interviews, 10+ pilot commitments and 100+ qualified waitlist prospects as baselines. **[anecdotal]** — [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/raise-seed-funding-pre-product-pre-revenue); [Ful.io](https://ful.io/blog/pre-revenue-saas-funding-firms)
- Climate and hardware: signed contracts with money attached rather than LOIs. — [Tech Nation](https://technation.io/how-to-scale-climate-tech-hardware-startup/)
- Healthtech: contracted revenue or LOIs beat projections, and clinical or regulatory progress is the substitute. — [VC Sheet](https://vcsheet.com/sheet/digital-health-funds)

### Inferences
- **Pre-revenue traction fields for the generator** (ordered by investor weight):
  1. Paid pilots ($, duration, conversion date).
  2. LOIs ($ value, counterparty name or logo, date signed, conversion timeline).
  3. Design partners (count, named, data access, usage frequency).
  4. Pre-orders or deposits ($).
  5. Channel or co-sell partnerships.
  6. Engagement of beta users (WAU, retention week 1/4/8).
  7. Qualified waitlist (size, % ICP, source).
  8. Customer discovery interviews (count).
  9. Shipping velocity and team credentials.
  10. Sector proofs: TRL, patents, grants, regulatory submissions, clinical data.
- The generator should display named, dated, dollar-valued evidence and down-weight raw waitlist counts.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study links LOI or pilot counts to seed success rates. The thresholds above (for example "10+ pilots") are anecdotal.
